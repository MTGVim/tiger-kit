# Research Evidence

Search current primary, official, production, OSS and academic evidence as applicable. Deep-read
the most relevant two or three cases, expanding only to resolve a concrete gap. Verify actual
source content, version/date, assumptions and deployment context; snippets, popularity and repeated
citations are not proof. Mark inaccessible or conflicting evidence and avoid invented measurements,
adoption or consensus. Generalize private context before external queries.

Compare applicable assumptions, accuracy, false positives/negatives, data needs, explainability,
operating cost, implementation effort and scaling conditions. Use decision-relevant dimensions
with unknowns explicit. Separate measured results, source claims and inference; prefer direct evidence
over source count. Preserve the simple/user approach as a baseline rather than only optimizing it.

Challenge the leading approach with targeted counterexamples, failure reports and limitations.
Resolve or retain disagreement, then recommend the minimum sufficient approach with confidence,
claim-adjacent sources and relevant revision/date. State what would justify more complexity.
If evidence cannot decide, name the smallest missing input or bounded validation spike with observable
success/failure criteria; proposing it grants no execution authority. Preserve verified partial
findings and mark the affected conclusion `Unverifiable` when required evidence is unavailable.
