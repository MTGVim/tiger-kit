# Targeted prerequisite checks

Use only for an important prerequisite that the current curriculum will not teach and that
affects the learner's goal. Compare actual lessons, assessments and inspected sources; do not
reclassify taught content or invent gaps from generic topic associations. Keep confirmed
dependencies separate from inferred ones and state what evidence would resolve uncertainty.
Prioritize by downstream goals, dependency reach and actual learner answers, without a graph,
fixed question count or invented numerical readiness score.

Read the identity-matching mission, learner profile, learning records and actual attempts first.
Reuse demonstrated knowledge and resolved misconceptions. A declared skill is a claim, an
unsubmitted answer is unknown, and a wrong answer is one observed attempt rather than proof of
general inability. Do not repeat a readiness or mission interview merely on resume.

When a check would change the next lesson, use the smallest concrete code/error/output or
reasoning problem that tests the dependency. Use plausible misconception-based alternatives,
one defensible correct answer and reasoning for each choice; split ambiguous questions. After
an actual response, explain the reasoning and offer targeted preparation/retry. If there is no
material untaught gap, continue the lesson without a diagnostic. Preserve no-interview requests.

Keep any justified sparse evidence in the existing learner-owned state under learning-state.md;
do not infer mastery from quiz totals, guessing, reading or generated content. Do not introduce
central scores, login, external submission or cross-course sharing. When preparing the next lesson,
carry relevant dependency evidence, observed answers and remaining uncertainty within authorized
scope; keep manual completion separate from tutoring evidence. HTML lessons must remain usable
offline without Node/Python/server/API installation. Existing question, source,
privacy, artifact and optional narration contracts still apply.
