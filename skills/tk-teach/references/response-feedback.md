# Feedback on Actual Attempts

Read for an actual answer/result, learner question, requested solution, retry, skip or stop.
Restore the same verified mission, relevant taught material and recent evidence first.
Use feedback to choose the next help or task; do not expose a grading taxonomy or numerical
ability score to the learner.

## Interpret what was observed

- Correct result with sound demonstrated reasoning: acknowledge the mechanism used. Offer
  a relevant changed-condition task or next concept; keep any learning claim within the
  observed scope. Do not escalate difficulty just to force an eventual wrong answer.
- Partially correct: acknowledge the valid part and target the missing condition or causal
  step with a narrower example/question. Do not restart already demonstrated material.
- Correct result with an incorrect explanation: explain the mismatch or ask for a concrete
  trace/variation that distinguishes the models. A lucky output does not establish the
  reasoning; do not advance its mastery record. With no reasoning shown, keep it unknown
  and request only the evidence that would change the next lesson.
- Wrong result: distinguish an observed error from a demonstrated false rule. Use a smaller
  worked case, counterexample or direct explanation matched to the actual gap, then offer
  a retry. Confidence or hesitation alone does not identify a misconception or inability.
- No answer, skipped work or artifact praise: retain unknown understanding. A next-step note
  may change, but no attempt, corrected misconception or proficiency is invented.

A relevant test result supports that check, not general understanding. Read actual submitted
code/reasoning and available outputs before deciding feedback; state execution that remains
unverified. Use existing executable-exercise verification for authored checks, not a new
assessment engine. A response can justify one small learning record only when its evidence
changes what is appropriate to teach next, under learning-state.md.

## Adapt without trapping the learner

Teach the needed knowledge before an attempt. Prefer a small hint or focused retry when
that serves the learner, but provide a requested solution or worked explanation. Explain
why it works and optionally offer a fresh transfer problem; do not count the provided
solution as independent success or force a fixed refusal ladder. If the learner is stuck,
re-explain or reduce the step rather than asking endlessly narrower questions.

A question about the current material can be answered without a new HTML or mission interview.
A requested stop ends the exercise: summarize only observed progress and unresolved points,
leave a next-step pointer when useful, and do not force another question. A skip can continue
to the learner's chosen material with prerequisite uncertainty visible; it does not certify
completion. Do not schedule reminders or start another session automatically.

## Keep offline and agent feedback distinct

An offline lesson may reveal reasoned answers or run a small local self-check. It cannot
make the agent observe clicks, typed reasoning or completion without an independently
requested supported channel. Invite the learner to send the attempt, reasoning or actual
output in conversation for personalized feedback. Do not create polling, background wakeups,
servers, external submissions or fabricated observations. Preserve the HTML and its answers
when only conversational feedback is needed; update lesson content only for an actual defect
or requested revision. Keep client progress separate from sparse tutoring evidence.
